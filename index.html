About AIMD (Additive Increase, Multiplicative Decrease)

TCP Congestion Avoidance

AIMD is the closed-loop feedback-control algorithm used by TCP to dynamically probe for available network bandwidth. The sender increases its transmission rate conservatively during successful rounds and backs off aggressively when congestion is detected.

Additive Increase (AI)
When all packets in an RTT are successfully acknowledged, cwnd increases linearly by α (+1 segment/RTT by default).
Multiplicative Decrease (MD)
When congestion occurs (candidate window ≥ threshold), cwnd is reduced multiplicatively by factor β (×0.5 by default).
cwnd Congestion Window: The maximum volume of data (in segments) the sender may transmit without waiting for an ACK.
RTT Round Trip Time: The round duration taken for packets to reach the receiver and for their ACKs to return.
α (Alpha) Additive Increase: Segments added to cwnd upon a congestion-free RTT round.
β (Beta) Multiplicative Factor: Window multiplier applied upon detecting congestion (0 < β < 1).
Threshold Congestion Limit: Window boundary triggering deterministic packet loss in this model.
ⓘ
Educational Model Note: This simulator implements a deterministic, threshold-based educational model of TCP AIMD congestion avoidance. It intentionally abstracts away slow start, retransmission timeouts (RTO), and triple duplicate ACKs to focus purely on the mathematical sawtooth principles of AIMD.
